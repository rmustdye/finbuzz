01 · Findingswhat the data actually shows

Three things I can defend in an interview

r = −0.151change correlation

Buzz and price moved independently

Comparing day-to-day changes (the honest method — raw levels produce spurious correlation), search attention and NVDA returns showed a weak, statistically insignificant relationship. Over the same window the stock quietly moved +5.8% while attention swung between 18 and 100.

nonelead-lag signal

Attention didn't predict tomorrow, either

I tested whether attention leads price by 1–5 days. 2 lags crossed the p < .05 line — with opposite signs. Six tests are expected to produce false positives by chance, and conflicting signs are the fingerprint of noise, not signal. A less careful analysis would have called this "predictive power."

±2pts between pulls

The measuring stick itself wobbles

My verification step compared two separate exports of the same Google Trends query: they disagreed on 31 of 59 dates (up to 2 points). Google Trends is sampled and re-normalised on every pull — a real limitation most beginner projects never notice, and exactly why this project publishes file hashes and pull dates with every chart.

03 · Insight reportthe write-up
FinBuzz insight · Nº 001Aug–Nov 2025

Attention is a mood ring, not a crystal ball

What happened. Between 25 August and 14 November 2025, US search interest in "AI stocks" was volatile — peaking at 100 on 7 October and dipping below 20 once — while NVDA moved from $128.74 to $136.37 (+5.8%). Day-to-day, the two series were statistically unrelated (r = −0.151, p = 0.258).

What it means. Over this window, broad retail curiosity was not a useful signal for the sector's flagship stock. That's consistent with how markets work: NVDA's price is set overwhelmingly by institutional flows and earnings expectations, while a generic search term captures news-reading, homework and hype — attention from people who mostly aren't trading.

The valuable output of a sentiment tracker is often a null result — knowing which signals to ignore is half of analysis.

What would make the signal stronger. Ticker-specific terms ("NVDA stock"), event windows around earnings dates, and volume-weighted comparison are the next iterations — each already supported by the pipeline.

What this is not. Not investment advice, not a trading system, and not a claim about any period outside the stated window. Correlation, even when found, is not causation.

04 · Method & limitationshow to check my work

Every number here is reproducible

  1. Sources. Attention: official Google Trends export (US, web search, 3-month window). Prices: Yahoo Finance daily adjusted close. Raw files are committed to the repo with SHA-256 fingerprints so anyone can verify they were never edited.
  2. Alignment. Series are inner-joined on date. Markets close on weekends and holidays but searching never stops, so 93 calendar days become 59 trading days — by design, not by accident.
  3. Statistics. Correlations are computed on daily percentage changes, not raw levels, to avoid the spurious correlation that trending series produce. The pipeline recomputes every correlation two independent ways and refuses to publish if they disagree.
  4. Known limitation — sampling variance. Google Trends scores are relative (100 = the window's peak) and re-sampled per request; two pulls of the identical query differed on 31 of 59 dates here. Sharp V-shaped dips that fully recover within days (detected automatically this window: Sep 11–Sep 15) are treated as probable sampling artifacts; sustained declines are treated as real falls in attention.
  5. Known limitation — keyword breadth. "AI stocks" measures interest in a theme, not in NVDA specifically. The pipeline supports keyword baskets for the next report.
  6. Multiple-comparison honesty. Six lag tests at p < .05 are expected to yield false positives; isolated significant lags with conflicting signs are reported as noise, not discoveries.
05 · Aboutwhy this exists

A self-assigned analyst internship

I built FinBuzz to do the work before anyone hired me to do it: collect real data, verify it, analyse it honestly, and publish the result — the weekly rhythm of a junior research or strategy analyst.

Everything on this page — the pipeline, the statistics, the charts, the write-up — was built from zero, documented so that every line can be explained, and designed to be re-run in twenty minutes with fresh data.

What this demonstrates

Data discipline — provenance logs, file hashing, integrity gates that block bad data from publishing.

Statistical judgment — % changes over levels, lag testing, and the restraint to call noise "noise."

Communication — findings a non-technical reader can follow, with the caveats a technical reader would demand.
