01 · Findingswhat the data actually shows

Three things I can defend in an interview

r = -0.179change correlation

Buzz and price moved independently

Comparing day-to-day changes, search attention and NVDA returns showed a weak, statistically insignificant relationship (p = 0.175). Attention hit 100 on June 2 and again on July 2. NVDA went from $208.03 to $207.29 - essentially flat.

nonelead-lag signal

Attention didn't predict tomorrow, either

I tested whether attention leads price by 1-5 days. Not a single lag reached p < .05. The closest was lag-5 at p = 0.071, but one near-miss out of six tests is exactly what chance produces.

+0.82 to -0.85rolling correlation swing

The relationship flipped completely

In early May, attention and price moved together (r = +0.82). By mid-July, they moved in opposite directions (r = -0.85). A real signal would hold steady. This one reversed itself - the signature of two unrelated series.

03 · Insight reportthe write-up
FinBuzz insight - No. 002Apr-Jul 2026

Attention peaked. Price shrugged.

What happened. Between 24 April and 21 July 2026, US search interest in "AI stocks" spiked twice to 100 (June 2 and July 2), then collapsed to the low 20s by mid-July. NVDA, meanwhile, ended almost exactly where it started: $208.03 to $207.29 (-0.4%). Day-to-day, the two series were statistically unrelated (r = -0.179, p = 0.175).

What it means. This window is especially telling because it contains a clear attention narrative - a rise, a double peak, a crash - while the stock stayed flat. If retail search interest were a price driver, this is exactly where you'd expect to see it. Instead, NVDA's price was set by institutional flows, earnings expectations, and broader market forces that have nothing to do with how many people Google "AI stocks."

The most interesting finding isn't the correlation. It's the rolling correlation flipping from +0.82 to -0.85 - proof that the relationship has no structural basis.

What would make the signal stronger. Ticker-specific terms ("NVDA stock"), event windows around earnings dates, and volume-weighted comparison are the next iterations.

What this is not. Not investment advice, not a trading system, and not a claim about any period outside the stated window. Correlation, even when found, is not causation.

04 · Method and limitationshow to check my work

Every number here is reproducible

  1. Sources. Attention: official Google Trends export (US, web search, 3-month custom range). Prices: yfinance adjusted close. Raw files hashed (SHA-256) and published.
  2. Alignment. Series are inner-joined on date. 89 calendar days become 60 trading days (weekends and holidays removed by design).
  3. Statistics. Correlations computed on daily percentage changes, not raw levels, to avoid spurious trending-series correlation. Every correlation recomputed two independent ways; pipeline refuses to publish if they disagree.
  4. Known limitation - sampling variance. Google Trends scores are relative (100 = that window's peak) and re-sampled per request. Different pulls of the same query can differ by a few points.
  5. Known limitation - keyword breadth. "AI stocks" measures interest in a theme, not NVDA specifically. The pipeline supports keyword baskets for future reports.
  6. Multiple-comparison honesty. Six lag tests at p < .05 are expected to yield ~0.3 false positives by chance. No lags reached significance in this window.
