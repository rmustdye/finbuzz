01 · Findingsreplicated across two windows

Four things I can defend in an interview

-0.151 / -0.179both windows

Buzz and price moved independently - twice

In window 1, NVDA gained 5.9% while attention faded. In window 2, NVDA was flat while attention spiked to 100 twice then crashed to the 20s. Both windows produced weak, non-significant correlations on daily changes. One window could be a fluke. Two is a pattern.

0 reliableacross 12 lag tests

Attention didn't predict tomorrow in either window

Window 1 produced two "significant" lags - with opposite signs (the fingerprint of noise). Window 2 produced zero. Twelve tests total, zero consistent predictive signals.

+0.84 to -0.85rolling correlation range

The relationship reversed itself completely

Both windows showed the rolling 14-day correlation swinging from strongly positive to strongly negative. In window 2, it went from +0.82 in May to -0.85 in July. A real signal doesn't flip sign every few weeks.

replicateddifferent conditions

The null result survived different market regimes

Window 1: NVDA rising (+5.9%), attention declining. Window 2: NVDA flat (-0.4%), attention spiking then crashing. Bull market, sideways market - the finding held. Most junior projects test once. This one tested twice.

03 · Insight reportthe write-up
FinBuzz combined insight2025 + 2026

Tested once, it's a finding. Tested twice, it's evidence.

Window 1 (Aug-Nov 2025). Search interest in "AI stocks" peaked at 100 in early October, then declined steadily to 18 by mid-November. NVDA, ignoring the fading buzz entirely, climbed from $128.74 to $136.37 (+5.9%). The daily-change correlation was r = -0.151, not significant.

Window 2 (Apr-Jul 2026). Attention told a different story - it started low, spiked to 100 twice (June 2 and July 2), then collapsed to the low 20s. NVDA ended almost exactly where it started: $208.03 to $207.29 (-0.4%). The daily-change correlation was r = -0.179, also not significant.

Two windows, two different attention narratives, two different market conditions, one consistent result: retail search buzz does not move this stock.

The rolling correlation is the sharpest evidence. In both windows, the 14-day rolling correlation swung from strongly positive to strongly negative. Window 2 is especially striking: it started at +0.82 in May (attention rising, price rising) and ended at -0.85 in July (attention falling, price recovering). A relationship that reverses every few weeks is not a relationship.

Why the null result matters. Knowing which signals to ignore is half of analysis. A system that correctly identifies "this popular indicator has no edge" is more useful than one that hallucinates patterns. FinBuzz's value isn't the correlation - it's the methodology that honestly reports the correlation.

What would change the conclusion. Ticker-specific keywords ("NVDA stock"), event windows around earnings, volume-weighted analysis, and testing across different asset classes. Each is supported by the pipeline.

What this is not. Not investment advice, not a trading system, and not a universal claim. These are two specific windows for one keyword and one stock. The finding is narrow, honest, and reproducible.

04 · Method and limitationshow to check my work

Every number is reproducible

  1. Sources. Window 1: Google Trends export + Yahoo Finance adjusted close. Window 2: Google Trends export + yfinance adjusted close. All raw files hashed (SHA-256) and committed to the repo.
  2. Alignment. Inner join on date. 93 calendar days became 59 trading days (window 1). 89 calendar days became 60 trading days (window 2).
  3. Statistics. Correlations on daily percentage changes, not raw levels. Each correlation computed two independent ways; pipeline refuses to publish if they disagree.
  4. Replication design. The two windows were chosen to be non-overlapping and separated by five months. Different market regimes (rising vs flat) make the null result more robust.
  5. Known limitation - sampling variance. Google Trends scores are relative (100 = window peak) and re-sampled per request. Window 1's cross-check found two pulls disagreed on 31 of 59 dates by up to 2 points.
  6. Known limitation - keyword breadth. "AI stocks" measures interest in a theme, not NVDA specifically.
  7. Multiple-comparison honesty. Twelve lag tests across two windows. Two crossed p < .05 in window 1 with opposite signs = noise. Zero crossed in window 2.

I built FinBuzz to do the work before anyone hired me to do it: collect real data, verify it, analyse it honestly, and publish the result.
