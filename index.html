03 Insight reportcombined analysis
FinBuzz combined insightJul 2025 - Jul 2026

Tested once, it's a finding. Tested three times, it's evidence.

The full year (weekly). From July 2025 to July 2026, "AI stocks" search interest sat in the 27-50 range for eight months, then exploded to 100 in late May/early June 2026 before crashing back to 27 by mid-July. NVDA, over the same period, climbed steadily from $171 to $203 (+18.8%). The weekly change correlation was r = -0.062 - essentially zero (p = 0.661).

Window 1 (Aug-Nov 2025, daily). Attention peaked at 100 in early October, then faded to 18 by mid-November. NVDA climbed from $129 to $136 (+5.9%). Daily-change correlation: r = -0.151, not significant.

Window 2 (Apr-Jul 2026, daily). Attention spiked to 100 twice (June 2 and July 2), then collapsed to the low 20s. NVDA ended almost exactly where it started: $208 to $207 (-0.4%). Daily-change correlation: r = -0.179, not significant.

Three windows, three different attention stories, three different market conditions, one consistent result: retail search buzz does not move this stock.

The rolling correlation tells the sharpest story. In every window, the relationship between attention and price flipped from positive to negative and back. Window 2 is the most dramatic: +0.82 in May (both rising together) to -0.85 in July (attention crashing while price recovered). A relationship that reverses every few weeks is not a relationship.

Why the null result matters. Knowing which signals to ignore is half of analysis. A system that honestly identifies "this popular indicator has no edge" is more useful than one that hallucinates patterns. FinBuzz's value isn't the correlation - it's the methodology that honestly reports the correlation.

What would change the conclusion. Ticker-specific keywords ("NVDA stock"), event windows around earnings, volume-weighted analysis, and testing across different asset classes.

What this is not. Not investment advice, not a trading system, and not a universal claim. These are three specific windows for one keyword and one stock. The finding is narrow, honest, and reproducible.

04 Method and limitationshow to check my work

Every number is reproducible

  1. Sources. Attention: official Google Trends exports (US, web search). Prices: Yahoo Finance adjusted close (window 1) and yfinance (windows 2 and 3). All raw files SHA-256 hashed.
  2. Alignment. Daily windows: inner join on date, weekends/holidays removed. Weekly window: each Sunday trend score matched to nearest trading day's close.
  3. Statistics. Correlations on percentage changes, not levels. Each computed two independent ways. Pipeline refuses to publish if they disagree.
  4. Replication design. Three non-overlapping analyses. Two daily (3-month) windows provide statistical rigor. One weekly (12-month) window provides the macro narrative. Different market regimes (rising, flat, rallying) strengthen the null result.
  5. Normalization disclosure. Google Trends scores are relative to each window's peak. A score of 100 in the weekly view ("peak of the year") represents a different absolute search volume than 100 in a daily view ("peak of 3 months"). Trend scores are not comparable across windows. This is by design: each window's statistics are computed internally.
  6. Sampling variance. Google Trends re-samples per request. Window 1's cross-check found two pulls disagreed on 31 of 59 dates by up to 2 points.
  7. Keyword breadth. "AI stocks" measures interest in a theme, not NVDA specifically.
  8. Multiple-comparison honesty. 16 lag tests across 3 windows. Two crossed p < .05 in window 1 with opposite signs = noise. Zero crossed in windows 2 or 3.
